$\Phi$ is $m$-strongly convex, meaning for some $m > 0$:

$$
\Phi(y) \ge \Phi(x) + \langle \nabla \Phi(x), y-x \rangle + \frac{m}{2}\|y-x\|^2
$$

This strong convexity implies that the gradient $\nabla \Phi$ is strongly monotone:

$$
\langle \nabla \Phi(x) - \nabla \Phi(y), x-y \rangle \ge m\|x-y\|^2

This property ensures that the potential has a unique minimum and provides a robust landscape for convergence.

### II. The Dissipative Term

The evolution of the system state $S$ is governed by an operator $\mathcal{T}(S)$, defined by a gradient descent-like update:

$$

This formulation directly leads to energy dissipation, as shown by the time derivative of $\Phi(S)$:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi(S) = -|\nabla \Phi(S)|^2 \le 0

This derivative is strictly negative for $S \neq S^*$ (where $S^*$ is the unique minimizer of $\Phi$). This means that any deviation from the optimal state produces an increase in potential energy, and the system's natural flow produces energy dissipation, anchoring it to the physical layer.

$$

$$

### Claim

$$

1. A static gradient descent algorithm minimizes $\Phi_0$, not $\tilde{\Phi}_t$. It converges to a fixed point $S^*$ where $\nabla \Phi_0(S^*) = 0$.

4. This implies that the real energy at the system's converged state $S^*$ is strictly greater than the minimum possible true alignment energy:
   $$
   \tilde{\Phi}_t(S^*) > \inf_S \tilde{\Phi}_t(S)

   A strictly positive alignment gap always exists.

### Consequence

The system converges to a point that is not a minimizer of the true grounding energy.
Thus:
* The real alignment energy never reaches zero.

* The energy floor remains strictly positive, indicating continuous, unavoidable energetic cost for misalignment.

This demonstrates the thermodynamic insolvency of Static IP systems.
